About

My research lies at the intersection of probability theory, analysis and geometry, with particular emphasis on heat kernel analysis, functional inequalities, and stochastic processes on Lie groups and sub-Riemannian manifolds.

I received my Ph.D. from Cornell University in 1998 under the supervision of Leonard Gross. Before joining the University of Rochester as the John F. Randolph Professor and Department Chair, I was a Professor at the University of Connecticut for more than two decades. I held a Bonn Research Chair at the Hausdorff Center for Mathematics (2023–2025) and have held research professorships at MSRI/SLMath (2022, 2025).

Research

My work develops analytic and probabilistic tools — Harnack inequalities, logarithmic Sobolev inequalities, heat kernel estimates — in settings where classical techniques break down: infinite-dimensional groups, sub-Riemannian geometries, and degenerate diffusions.

Stochastic AnalysisDiffusions and Brownian motion on Lie groups, infinite-dimensional stochastic processes, quasi-invariance of heat kernel measures
Functional InequalitiesLogarithmic Sobolev and Poincaré inequalities, Harnack inequalities on sub-Riemannian and Riemannian manifolds
Heat Kernel AnalysisHeat kernels on infinite-dimensional groups, Heisenberg and nilpotent Lie groups, hypoelliptic operators
Sub-Riemannian GeometrySubelliptic operators, Carnot groups, sub-Laplacians, curvature-dimension conditions in non-smooth spaces
Infinite-Dimensional GroupsHilbert–Schmidt and infinite-dimensional Heisenberg groups, holomorphic functions, Taylor isomorphisms
Langevin & Ergodic TheoryConvergence of Langevin dynamics with singular potentials, gamma calculus, Ornstein–Uhlenbeck perturbations
